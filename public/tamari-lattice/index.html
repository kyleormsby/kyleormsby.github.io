Drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Hover an element to see its covers; click to pin it. The elements are the Cn+1 full binary trees with n+2 leaves, equivalently the transfer systems on the chain [n], equivalently the noncrossing partitions of [n+1]; the three buttons redraw the same set. Trees and transfer systems carry the Tamari order: a right rotation (xy)z → x(yz) on trees is exactly adding relations to a transfer system, so those two drawings share one Hasse diagram. Noncrossing partitions carry the Kreweras order, refinement: coarser than Tamari (every Kreweras relation is a Tamari relation, not conversely), graded by the number of blocks, while Tamari is not graded — its rows here count the relations of the transfer system (for a tree: the pairs of a node and a node in its right subtree; for the Dyck path: the area), a statistic every cover increases, sometimes by more than one. Color is the number of blocks, which is also the number of peaks of the Dyck path and the number of tree nodes whose right child is a leaf.

Made for Math 372: Combinatorics at Reed College, fall 2026. The Cn+1 elements are drawn three ways: as full binary trees with n+2 leaves, as transfer systems on the chain [n] (Balchin, Barnes and Roitzheim, N∞-operads and associahedra, showed these form the Tamari lattice under inclusion), and as noncrossing partitions of [n+1]. The first two carry Tamari's order, rotation (Tamari 1962; the lattice property is Huang–Tamari 1972); the third carries Kreweras' refinement order (1971), which is coarser: Bernardi and Bonichon (Intervals in Catalan lattices and realizers of triangulations, 2009) fix the bijections used here, under which every Kreweras relation is a Tamari relation and every Tamari relation is a Stanley relation on Dyck paths.
