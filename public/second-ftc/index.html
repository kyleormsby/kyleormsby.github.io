The Second FTC

A(x) = ∫ax f(t) dt  ⇒  A′(x) = f(x)

what is this?

The top graph is f. The shaded region from a to x is the accumulation A(x) = ∫ax f(t) dt: area above the axis counts positive (rust), below counts negative (blue). The bottom graph is A itself, traced out as x moves.

The highlighted strip from x to x + h is the area added when x steps forward by h, so its area is A(x + h) − A(x): the rise of the secant in the bottom graph. The dashed box over it is a rectangle of height f(x) and width h. For small h the strip is nearly that rectangle, so

A(x + h) − A(x) ≈ f(x) · h,   so   [A(x + h) − A(x)] / h → f(x).

The left side is the slope of the secant on A; its limit is A′(x). Shrink h and watch the secant turn into the tangent line, whose slope is the height of f. A negative h is not offered, but the same picture works from the left.

Things to notice: A rises where f > 0 and falls where f < 0; A has a peak or valley where f changes sign; changing a shifts A up or down without changing its shape (another “+ C”). For e−t² there is no formula for A, but the theorem still says A′(x) = e−x².

Drag in either graph to move x. Keys: ← → move x, ↑ ↓ change h, space sweeps. Functions use t (or x); the address bar keeps the settings.
