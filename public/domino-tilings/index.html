what is this?

A domino tiling covers the m × n board with 1 × 2 tiles, each lying on one dark and one light square. The big number counts them all.

Fibonacci. A tiling of the 2 × n strip begins with one upright domino or with two flat ones, so the count for n is the sum of the counts for n − 1 and n − 2.

Trigonometry. Kasteleyn and Temperley–Fisher (1961) showed that the count squared is |det K| for K = An ⊗ 1 + i · 1 ⊗ Am, where An is the adjacency matrix of a path. Its eigenvalues are 2 cos(kπ/(n+1)): shadows of evenly spaced points on a circle of radius 2. So the eigenvalues of K form the grid in the right-hand picture, and the count is the product of the squared distances from the origin to the grid points in the shaded quadrant.

Flips. Click a 2 × 2 block made of two parallel dominoes to rotate it. Any two tilings of a rectangle are joined by flips (Thurston); a flip changes the height function at one vertex by 4. Random tiling draws an exactly uniform sample with the transfer matrix; shuffle runs the flip random walk.
