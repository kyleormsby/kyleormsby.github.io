Rods are the four 2-torsion points of T²: 0=(0,0) at ∞, 1=(½,0), 2=(½,½), 3=(0,½). A loop on the sphere enclosing rods i and j lifts to a closed geodesic of T² parallel to the segment pipj, offset by ε (the disk around the two branch points lifts to an annulus); pulling it n times gives the closed geodesic of slope Mn(pj−pi). "Fill lines" draws nested loops at offsets ε·k/K so the taffy reads as a band. The arc itself is the ε = 0 limit. Rod 0 sits at ∞ (the north pole) and is fixed by every M: for the 3-rod pullers it is the extra puncture of S0,4, for Thibodeau's 4-rod puller it is a rod.

What you are seeing

The involution ι(z) = −z on the torus T² = ℂ/Λ has four fixed points, and the Weierstrass function ℘ : T² → ℂ∪{∞} is exactly the quotient map T² → T²/ι, a sphere branched over ℘(0) = ∞ and the three half-period values e₁, e₂, e₃. A linear map φ(x) = Mx on T² commutes with ι, so it descends through ℘ to a map of the sphere that permutes the four branch points — the rods of a taffy puller. The taffy is a loop around two rods; its lift is a closed geodesic of the torus, and the puller just changes its slope (Thiffeault, The mathematics of taffy pullers).

To make the pulling continuous we run the one-parameter group Ms: at time s the strand is Msγ in the plane, the lattice is Λs = MsΛ, and the picture is ℘Λs(Msz). At integer s the lattice is back to Λ and the picture is the s-th iterate of the puller; in between, the branch points ei(Λs) move and come back permuted — that is the rod motion. Strand length grows like λs, λ the dilatation.
