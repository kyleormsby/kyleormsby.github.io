This site has a new home at [e-infinity.space](https://e-infinity.space) — the third address it has had, and with luck the last for a while. It started on WordPress as [The Bright Obvious](/writing/until-the-bright-obvious-stands-motionless-in-cold/), moved to a Jekyll site on github.io in 2021, and has now been rebuilt from the ground up.

About the name: it's like e-mail, but for infinity. Also a space with a multiplication that is associative and commutative up to coherent higher homotopy.

The pun turned out to be load-bearing in a way I hadn't planned. The animation on the front page is a loop in $\mathrm{Conf}_{11}(\mathbb{R}^2)$ — eleven points stirring a rectangle, never colliding, spelling out a braid word as they go. But the little 2-disks operad satisfies <span style="white-space:nowrap">$E_2(k) \simeq \mathrm{Conf}_k(\mathbb{R}^2)$</span>, so that picture is really a loop in $E_2(11)$, and its fundamental group is the pure braid group $P_{11}$. Which is exactly why $E_2$-algebras are braided rather than symmetric. Let $n \to \infty$: the spaces become contractible, the braiding collapses into symmetry, and what survives is $E_\infty$. The braid on the front page is precisely the structure that dies in the limit the site is named after.

## What's here

**[Visualizations](/viz/).** Nineteen of them, all running in your browser and phoning home to nobody: closed geodesics on the space of unimodular 3-lattices, a free group of rank two sitting inside $SO(3)$, nets of four-dimensional polytopes, and — newest — the [Schwartz pentagram map](/pentagram-map/), which on a pentagon returns you to a projectively equivalent copy of where you began, so the picture falls inward forever.

**[Teaching](/teaching/).** Every course I've taught, forty-three of them going back to Michigan in 2006. The recent ones have full schedules; the older ones link out to wherever they still live.

**[Writing](/writing/).** Everything since 2013, including the whole Bright Obvious archive, which had been stranded on WordPress. Old permalinks still resolve — if you have a link to something of mine from 2021, it will land in the right place.

That archive is not embalmed, either. While moving it I got a lovely email from Nathan Smith, a senior at Brown, explaining that my answer to the follow-up puzzle in [Aha! A percolating pandemic and a perfect proof](/writing/aha/) was wrong — I had answered a question adjacent to the one I asked. He was right, and the post now says so.

There is a light/dark switch in the corner, the whole thing is fast and static, and nothing here tracks you.
